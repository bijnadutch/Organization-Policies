## 1. Purpose

This Policy defines XXAXX's approach to building and maintaining a security-aware workforce, from general cyber hygiene for all personnel through role-specific technical training for those with elevated responsibilities, including the management body itself. Well-informed people are one of XXAXX's strongest defenses against social engineering, phishing, and human error — and a workforce that doesn't know what "good" looks like cannot be expected to consistently practice it.

## 2. Scope

This Policy applies to all XXAXX employees, contractors, and temporary staff, and — for governance-level training obligations — the management body. It covers general security awareness training, role-specific technical training, phishing/social engineering simulation, and management body training on cybersecurity risk oversight.

## 3. Definitions

- **Security Awareness Training**: General training ensuring all personnel understand baseline security expectations, common threats, and how to respond to them.
- **Role-Specific Training**: Additional, more technical training tailored to individuals whose role carries elevated security responsibility (e.g., developers, system administrators, incident responders).
- **Phishing Simulation**: A controlled, simulated phishing exercise used to measure and improve susceptibility to social engineering.
- **Cyber Hygiene**: The set of routine practices (e.g., password hygiene, software updates, safe browsing) that reduce everyday cybersecurity risk.

## 4. Policy Statement

### 4.1 General Security Awareness Training

All personnel complete general security awareness training at onboarding (before or promptly upon gaining system access) and at least annually thereafter. Training covers, at minimum: phishing and social engineering recognition, password and authentication hygiene, safe handling of Confidential/Restricted data, acceptable use of XXAXX assets, incident reporting procedures, secure use of removable media and cloud services, and physical security basics (clear desk/screen, visitor handling, tailgating).

### 4.2 Role-Specific Training

Personnel in roles carrying elevated security responsibility receive additional, role-specific training proportionate to their function, including as applicable: secure coding training for developers (per the Secure Development Policy), incident response training for the Security Incident Response Team, privileged access and secure administration practices for system administrators, and secure procurement/due diligence practices for personnel involved in supplier management. Role-specific training is refreshed periodically and following significant changes to relevant threats or tooling.

### 4.3 Management Body Training

Members of XXAXX's management body complete training sufficient to identify and assess cybersecurity risks and evaluate risk-management practices in relation to their governance responsibilities, at induction to the role and periodically thereafter. This training addresses the management body's accountability for cybersecurity risk management, key elements of the risk landscape relevant to XXAXX's sector, and their oversight role during major incidents.

### 4.4 Phishing and Social Engineering Simulation

XXAXX conducts periodic, unannounced phishing simulation exercises across the workforce to measure susceptibility and reinforce training. Personnel who fail a simulation receive prompt, constructive follow-up training rather than punitive action, except in cases of repeated, unaddressed failure following prior remediation, which may be escalated per the HR Security Policy's disciplinary process. Aggregate (non-punitive, trend-focused) results are reported to the CISO and management to track organizational susceptibility over time.

### 4.5 Targeted Campaigns and Threat-Driven Awareness

In addition to the standing training program, XXAXX issues targeted awareness communications in response to emerging threats relevant to its sector or observed within its own environment (e.g., a new phishing campaign theme, a significant supply chain incident in the news), coordinated with the Risk Management Policy's threat intelligence activity.

### 4.6 Training Completion Tracking

Completion of mandatory training (general and role-specific) is tracked centrally, with reminders issued for outstanding completions and non-completion escalated to line managers and, where persistent, HR. Training completion status may factor into access provisioning decisions for roles where training is a prerequisite (e.g., privileged access is not granted or is suspended pending completion of required training).

### 4.7 Training Content Currency and Effectiveness

Training content is reviewed and updated at least annually to reflect the evolving threat landscape, lessons learned from incidents (per the Incident Management Policy's post-incident review), and changes to this policy suite. The effectiveness of the awareness program is periodically assessed (e.g., through phishing simulation trends, incident data linked to human error, and training feedback) rather than measured by completion rates alone.

### 4.8 New and Emerging Roles

Individuals moving into a role with elevated security responsibility complete the relevant role-specific training before or promptly upon assuming the new responsibilities, coordinated with the HR Security Policy's role-change provisions.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| CISO | Owns this Policy; defines training content and role-specific requirements; oversees phishing simulation program; reports effectiveness metrics to management. |
| Management Body | Completes governance-level cybersecurity training; sponsors and models a security-aware culture. |
| HR | Coordinates training assignment as part of onboarding and role-change processes; supports completion tracking and escalation. |
| Line Managers | Ensure their teams complete required training on time; reinforce awareness messaging. |
| Information Security Team | Delivers or coordinates delivery of training content; conducts phishing simulations; analyzes results and trends. |
| All Employees & Contractors | Complete required training within defined timelines; apply learned practices in daily work; report suspected phishing or social engineering attempts. |

## 6. Compliance and Enforcement

Non-completion of mandatory training within defined timelines is escalated per Section 4.6 and may affect access provisioning. Compliance with this Policy is monitored through training completion tracking, phishing simulation metrics, and internal audit of training records.

## 7. Exceptions

Exceptions to training timelines (e.g., extended leave) must be approved by the relevant line manager and HR, with a defined catch-up deadline upon return.

## 8. Review and Update

This Policy and the associated training curriculum are reviewed at least annually and upon significant change to the threat landscape, regulatory requirements, or organizational structure.
