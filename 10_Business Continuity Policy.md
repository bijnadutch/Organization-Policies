## 1. Purpose

This Policy establishes XXAXX's approach to ensuring the continuity of its critical business processes and services in the event of a disruptive incident — whether caused by a cyberattack, hardware or infrastructure failure, natural hazard, pandemic, loss of key personnel, or third-party/supplier failure. It ensures XXAXX can maintain, or rapidly restore, its essential and important services within acceptable timeframes, minimizing harm to service recipients and to XXAXX itself.

## 2. Scope

This Policy applies to all business processes, information systems, facilities, and third-party dependencies that support XXAXX's critical operations and its essential/important services. It applies to all employees and relevant third parties involved in business continuity planning, crisis management, and disaster recovery execution. Detailed technical recovery procedures for IT systems and data are addressed in the Backup & Disaster Recovery Policy, which operates as an implementing document under this Policy.

## 3. Definitions

- **Business Continuity Management (BCM)**: The holistic management process that identifies potential threats and their impact on business operations, and provides a framework for building organizational resilience.
- **Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**: The process of determining the criticality of business processes and the impact of their disruption over time.
- **Recovery Time Objective (RTO)**: The maximum acceptable length of time a process or system may be unavailable following a disruption.
- **Recovery Point Objective (RPO)**: The maximum acceptable amount of data loss, measured in time, following a disruption.
- **Maximum Tolerable Downtime (MTD)**: The maximum time a business process can be disrupted before causing unacceptable harm to XXAXX or its service recipients.
- **Business Continuity Plan (BCP)**: A documented plan describing how XXAXX will maintain or restore critical business processes during and after a disruption.
- **Crisis Management Team (CMT)**: The senior group responsible for strategic decision-making during a major disruptive event.

## 4. Policy Statement

### 4.1 Business Continuity Management Framework

XXAXX maintains a Business Continuity Management framework comprising: business impact analysis, continuity strategy selection, documented business continuity plans, crisis management arrangements, and a regular testing and exercise program. This framework is proportionate to XXAXX's size, the criticality of its services, and the likelihood and severity of disruptive scenarios.

### 4.2 Business Impact Analysis

XXAXX conducts a Business Impact Analysis at least annually and following significant organizational change, to identify critical business processes, the systems and third parties they depend on, and the impact of disruption over time. For each critical process, the BIA establishes an RTO, RPO (where applicable), and Maximum Tolerable Downtime, approved by the relevant Process Owner and reviewed by the CISO/Head of Operations.

### 4.3 Continuity Strategy

For each critical process, XXAXX defines a continuity strategy proportionate to its RTO/MTD, which may include: redundant infrastructure or failover capability, alternative working locations or remote working arrangements, manual workaround procedures, alternative suppliers, or accepted temporary suspension where impact is tolerable. Continuity strategies for IT systems and data are implemented through the technical measures defined in the Backup & Disaster Recovery Policy.

### 4.4 Business Continuity Plans

Documented Business Continuity Plans are maintained for all critical business processes, covering: activation criteria and triggers, roles and responsibilities during activation, step-by-step recovery actions, required resources (people, systems, facilities, third parties), and stand-down/return-to-normal criteria. BCPs are kept current, accessible to relevant personnel (including in a form usable if primary systems are unavailable), and version-controlled.

### 4.5 Crisis Management

For disruptions of sufficient severity, XXAXX activates a Crisis Management Team responsible for strategic decision-making, resource allocation, stakeholder communication, and coordination with the incident/major incident response led under the Incident Management Policy. The CMT operates from a defined crisis management process, including escalation criteria, communication protocols, and a designated alternate decision-maker for each key role to ensure continuity of leadership.

### 4.6 Dependencies on Third Parties

Business continuity planning explicitly considers dependencies on critical suppliers and service providers identified under the Supplier & Third-Party Security Policy, including the continuity and disaster recovery capability of Tier 1 suppliers, and, where feasible, contingency arrangements (e.g., alternative providers, contractual continuity commitments) for single points of failure.

### 4.7 Testing and Exercising

Business Continuity Plans and crisis management arrangements are tested at least annually through a combination of tabletop exercises, walkthroughs, and, for the most critical processes, live or simulated failover tests. Testing scope increases proportionate to process criticality. Test outcomes, including identified gaps, are documented, and remediation actions are tracked to closure.

### 4.8 Communication During Disruption

Business continuity plans include predefined communication protocols for informing employees, customers/service recipients, suppliers, and, where a disruption also constitutes a Significant Incident, regulatory authorities in coordination with the NIS2 Incident Reporting Procedure.

### 4.9 Plan Activation and Post-Event Review

BCP activation follows defined triggers and is authorized by the Crisis Management Team or a designated deputy. Following any BCP activation, a post-event review is conducted to assess the effectiveness of the response and update plans, strategies, and RTO/RPO assumptions accordingly.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Management Body | Approves this Policy and overall continuity strategy; is a standing member or is briefed during Crisis Management Team activation for major disruptions. |
| CISO / Head of Operations | Owns this Policy; oversees the BIA process, BCP maintenance, and testing program. |
| Crisis Management Team | Makes strategic decisions during major disruptive events; coordinates cross-functional response and external communication. |
| Process Owners | Provide input to the BIA; maintain and test the BCP for their process; execute recovery actions during activation. |
| IT / Information Security Team | Implements and maintains technical recovery capability per the Backup & Disaster Recovery Policy; supports BCP testing for IT-dependent processes. |
| All Employees | Are familiar with relevant BCP elements for their role; participate in exercises as required. |
| Supplier/Procurement Function | Assesses continuity capability of critical suppliers; maintains contingency arrangements where feasible. |

## 6. Compliance and Enforcement

Failure to maintain a current BCP for a critical process, or failure to complete required testing, is logged as a risk under the Risk Management Policy and escalated to the CISO/Head of Operations. Compliance is monitored through BIA/BCP currency tracking, test completion records, and internal audit.

## 7. Exceptions

Exceptions to standard BCM requirements (e.g., a deferred test date due to resource constraints) must be approved by the CISO/Head of Operations, documented with rationale and a revised timeline, and tracked to closure.

## 8. Review and Update

This Policy, the BIA, and all Business Continuity Plans are reviewed at least annually, following any BCP activation, and upon significant change to XXAXX's critical processes, technology environment, or supplier base.
